1. What the product does

A small contractor cannot answer the phone while he is under a sink. Roughly a quarter of his calls go unanswered, and the ones that land in voicemail usually never come back. HandyLine AI answers every call, decides whether the job is one he actually does and one he actually reaches, gives an honest price range, and escalates the genuine emergencies to his mobile in seconds.

AI line Human line Decision point Problem in the old flow Outcome
CapabilityWhat actually happens
Answers 24/7 Every inbound call is picked up in about two seconds, including nights, weekends and holidays.
Qualifies before quoting Captures the issue and the caller's ZIP, checks it against the contractor's actual trade list and 25-mile service area, and only then gives a price.
Prices honestly Reads from the contractor's own price list. No trip fee inside the service area. Every quote ends with “final price confirmed once we see the job”.
Escalates emergencies Burst pipes, gas smells, smoke, shop HVAC failures ring the contractor's mobile immediately, retrying at 5 then 15 minutes, then text him.
Records everything Audio is copied into permanent storage, transcribed, summarised into structured notes, and written to the dashboard.
Two lines, one dashboard A dedicated number routes to a person; the AI number stays AI. Both write to the same work-order table, filterable by who answered.
The core idea: the AI is not trying to sound human. It is trying to be useful at 2am — qualify the job, price it honestly, and get out of the way. Anything a machine should not decide gets escalated to a person within seconds.

4. Step by step: the AI line

Each step, what it produces, and what it replaces.

1The call is answered
  • Picked up in roughly two seconds, at any hour. Opening line: “Handy Works service desk, what's the issue?”
  • Texas one-party consent is satisfied by a recording notice played at the start of every call.
Replaces: voicemail, missed calls, and the caller giving up after two rings.
2Issue and location are captured
  • What is broken, and which ZIP code. Two facts are enough to run every downstream decision.
  • The system is deliberately terse — short sentences, no filler — because a receptionist who rambles loses callers.
Replaces: the phone tag where Alex asks questions the customer has already answered once.
3Trade, service area and price are checked together
  • Is the issue one of the contractor's 15 trade lines? Is the ZIP inside his 25-mile radius from Bellaire?
  • If yes, the price range is read from his own price list, not a generic table. Furniture assembly starts at $75, TV mounting at $90, smart locks at $80.
  • No trip fee inside the service area. Outside greater Houston there is a $45 flat fee, and it is not credited against the work.
Replaces: “is this my trade?” and “am I going out of my way?” — the two questions that used to cost Alex real drive time.
4Out-of-scope work is declined honestly
  • Pest control, appliances, pool service, tree removal, full kitchen gut, gas lines, central AC installation, panel upgrades.
  • Where a trusted partner exists, the caller is offered the referral rather than simply being turned away.
  • Certified electrical and plumbing beyond minor work is referred out, because that is how Alex actually operates.
Replaces: Alex walking into a slab leak he should never have taken.
5Emergencies short-circuit everything
  • Gas smell, burst pipe, whole-house power loss, smoke or sparks, business HVAC failure with stock at risk, no heat with children or elderly at home.
  • The AI gives the safety instruction first — windows, leave, 911 — then rings Alex's mobile.
  • Retries at 5 minutes, then 15. After three misses it texts him and asks the caller to leave a message.
Replaces: an emergency trapped in the same voicemail box as a quote request.
6The call is written into the dashboard
  • Audio stored permanently — the provider link that expires after 30 minutes is copied into the project's own storage first.
  • Transcript, summary, caller intent, follow-up priority and recommended next action are all written to one record.
  • Alex sees it within a minute of the call ending.
Replaces: a conversation that existed only in two people's heads.

5. Step by step: the human line

Some customers will only talk to a person. Rather than fight that, the product gives them a number where a person genuinely answers — and keeps the paperwork.

1A separate number is published
  • One number for the AI, one for a human. Nothing is shared, so neither degrades the other.
  • Alex's own number and Abel's are whitelisted — they ring straight through and are never turned away by the system.
2His handset rings
  • Twenty seconds, then it gives up. The recording starts only when he actually answers, so an unanswered caller never hears a compliance notice.
  • Both sides of the conversation are recorded on separate channels.
Design note: the handset number is stored separately from the line number. An early version reused one field for both and the telephony was made to ring the line back on itself — caught only by reading the generated markup.
3If he picks up, the machine does the typing
  • The call is transcribed, then summarised into the same structured notes the AI line produces: intent, key facts, action items, follow-up priority.
  • Alex spends the call talking to a customer instead of writing down what he promised.
Replaces: the sticky note that says “Sarah, faucet, call back Mon”.
4If nobody picks up
  • The caller gets voicemail, and the call is still recorded and summarised.
  • A callback work order appears in the dashboard flagged high priority, so it cannot quietly disappear.
  • The behaviour is configurable per customer: offer an instant quote instead, or simply apologise and end the call.
5Both kinds of call appear together
  • The dashboard carries an “Answered by” filter — all lines, AI, or human.
  • Human-handled calls are badged so they are distinguishable at a glance.
  • There is one work-order table, not two. Customer, transcript, recording, follow-up and status columns are shared, so nothing downstream has to care who picked up.

Why this matters commercially: the point is not that the AI is clever. It is that a lead which would have been lost in a voicemail box now arrives with a price range, a transcript and a priority — before the contractor has finished his current job.

Design decisions worth knowing

DecisionReasoning
AI, not human, on the main lineA machine is cheaper than a missed lead. The human line exists for customers who insist, not as the default.
Twilio records the human lineOnly the provider actually carrying the call can guarantee recording. The human line avoids depending on unverified behaviour.
One work-order tableA separate table for human calls would duplicate every field and force two code paths for no benefit.
Audio copied on arrivalProvider links die in 30 minutes. Copying immediately is the difference between a permanent record and a broken link.
“general” is a real tradeAll four AI tools fall back to it. Without it, unclassified calls fail the scope check and get wrongly declined.
Never promise a final priceThe quote is a range plus “confirmed once we see the job”. This protects the contractor from a phone-quoted price he cannot honour.
